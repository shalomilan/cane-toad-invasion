How to read it

  • A record is not the same as a toad. A record needs toads to be there and someone to report them. The model separates both: toads arrive (depending on distance, neighbours, climate and region) and are then detected (depending on how many people record frogs there). Where few people look, toads can be present for years without a record.
  • Test years (2021–2025) show the probability of a record, which is what can be compared with reality. Forecast years (2026–2035) show the probability that toads are present, recorded or not. That is why the area jumps in 2026: it starts counting toads that are there but not yet recorded.
  • Assumption: once toads arrive in a cell, they stay. This may fail at the dry margins, where toads are recorded less often than expected.
  • Desert limit: the same model, but desert cells (Köppen BW) cannot be colonised.
  • The western front is the third westernmost occupied cell west of 141°E; its advance is measured from its position in the first year with at least three occupied cells west of 141°E.
  • Records for 2025 are probably incomplete (upload lag in the Atlas of Living Australia).

Data: Atlas of Living Australia, GBIF, WorldClim 2.1, HydroRIVERS. Model: discrete-time survival (logistic regression) on a cell × year table, simulated 50 (test) and 200 (forecast) times, with survey effort near the invaded range as a predictor. Code and notebooks.
